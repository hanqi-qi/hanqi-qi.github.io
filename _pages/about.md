## Short Bio

**Hanqi Yan** is an Assistant Professor (UK lecturer) at [King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/people/hanqi-yan) (hanqi.yan@kcl.ac.uk), Department of Informatics, affiliated with the [NLP group](https://kclnlp.github.io/index.html).  Before that, she was a postdoctoral researcher in the same department (2024-2025). She mainly focuses on interpretability and alignment for language models on various reasoning tasks, with an emphasis on uncovering the underlying mechanisms that drive model behaviour. She serves as Area Chair/Senior PC for ACL, EMNLP, COLM and AAAI. She was selected for [AAAI27 New Faculty Highlights](https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-27/new-faculty-highlights-call-for-proposals/). She delivered a [AAAI26-tutorial](https://srl4llm.github.io/) about "structured representation learning for LLM interpretability, generalisability and transferability", organized workshops at AACL2022 and AACL26. She obtained her Ph.D from the University of Warwick(2024), Msc and BEng from Peking University and Beihang University.

## Research Highlights
My research aims to uncover the internal mechanisms of language models and use that understanding to build models that are more reliable, faithful, and safe. Specifically, we are interested in the following research topics:
- How do LLMs represent and process knowledge internally — and where do they break down?  We study the internal workings of language models to identify how knowledge is encoded [[ICLR26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00544v1)][[ACL26finding](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=oVSQIwRwqs)], and how (unreliable) reasoning behaviours emerge [[SocialHack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04075)], [[PreactBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09890v1)] [[Neurips26-ToolCall](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09624v1)], [[ICLR26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00544v1)] [[ICLR26Blog](https://iclr-blogposts.github.io/2026/blog/2026/misalign-failure-mode/)], [[EMNLP24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17969)], [[UAI22Spotlight](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v180/yan22b.html)]

- How can mechanistic understanding translate into models that are faithful, efficient, and trustworthy? We apply mechanistic insights to improve model behaviour — faithfully correct[[COLM26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02830)] [[EMNLP26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24780)] [[ACL25](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=bOFGdbXdKV)], reasoning efficiency [[EMNLP25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21074)][[ICML26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06161)][[Neurips26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03784)], reasoning diversity [[ICML25Spotlight](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=Pp90xRxITT)][[ACL24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14963)], distribution shifts [[NeurIPS23](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/afda6bf3fb086eabbaf161ba1cec5a9a-Paper-Conference.pdf)],[[ACL21Oral](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.261.pdf)] and conceptual meta-reasoning framework [[ICML25Position](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/40142)] 

On the application side, I mostly focus on LLMs for AutoResearch: [[KDD26](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3770855.3818195)], [[COLM25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00255)], [[EMNLP25Demo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15706)] [[SocialVerse2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24911)]
